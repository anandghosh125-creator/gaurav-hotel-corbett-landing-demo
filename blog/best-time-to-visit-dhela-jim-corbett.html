Best Time to Visit Dhela Zone in Jim Corbett National Park

Everything you need to know about Dhela’s year-round wilderness, seasonal tiger movement, and planning your Gypsy safari from Aranya Corbett.

Unlike Dhikala or Bijrani—which close during the monsoon months—the Dhela Eco-Tourism Zone on the southern edge of Jim Corbett National Park remains open throughout the year (weather permitting). Known for its sweeping grasslands, dense Sal and Haldu forests, and tranquil waterholes, Dhela offers one of the highest biodiversity densities in the reserve.

Season-by-Season Breakdown

1. Winter (November to February) — Best for Birding & Crisp Mornings

Winter brings misty dawns, clear Shivalik views, and hundreds of migratory birds along Dhela’s streams. Temperatures range from 5°C at dawn to a pleasant 22°C by afternoon. Tigers often bask in sunlit clearings along the main safari tracks.

2. Summer (March to June) — Peak Tiger Sightings

As temperatures climb between 30°C and 40°C, dry foliage opens up long sightlines through the forest. Wildlife congregates around Dhela’s perennial waterholes, making late March through mid-June the premier window for tracking Bengal tigers, sloth bears, and wild elephant herds.

3. Monsoon (July to October) — Emerald Wilderness

While core zones like Dhikala shut down, Dhela stays accessible unless heavy rain temporarily closes forest tracks. Expect dramatic monsoon clouds, lush greenery, and serene, uncrowded trails.

Essential Dhela Safari Tips

  • Book Permits Early: Dhela allows a limited number of 4x4 Gypsies per shift (morning & afternoon). Reserve permits 30–45 days ahead for weekends.
  • Morning vs. Afternoon Shift: Dawn drives offer fresh pugmarks and active predators; golden-hour afternoon drives are ideal for photography around waterholes.
  • What to Carry: Neutral-toned clothing, a light fleece for early-morning open-jeep rides, binoculars (8x42), and a valid government photo ID matching your permit.
